# Know your rights: fair chance hiring in California and Los Angeles

**Last reviewed: 2026-09-01**

> This page is general information, not legal advice. Laws change and every
> situation is different. Talk to a lawyer or a legal aid organization about
> your own case.

## Employers usually cannot ask about your record up front

Under California's Fair Chance Act, most employers with 5 or more employees
cannot ask about your conviction history until after they have made you a
conditional job offer.

That means:

- Job applications should not ask about convictions.
- Interviewers should not ask about convictions before an offer.
- A background check should not run before a conditional offer.

## What happens after a conditional offer

If an employer runs a background check after offering you the job and then
wants to take the offer back because of what they found, they have to do an
individualized assessment. They must consider:

- How serious the conduct was.
- How much time has passed since it happened.
- Whether it has anything to do with the job you were offered.

## If they decide to withdraw the offer

The employer must send you a written notice that identifies the conviction
they are concerned about, includes a copy of the background check report, and
gives you at least 5 business days to respond.

You can use that time to:

- Point out mistakes in the report.
- Send evidence of rehabilitation: certificates, letters, work history,
  treatment records, anything that shows who you are now.
- Explain circumstances the employer does not know about.

If you tell them within those 5 days that you are disputing the report, you
get at least 5 more business days to gather your evidence.

## Things employers cannot consider at all

- Arrests that did not lead to a conviction.
- Convictions that were sealed, dismissed, expunged, or eradicated.
- Participation in a diversion or deferred entry of judgment program.
- Most convictions from juvenile court.

## Los Angeles adds more protections

The City of Los Angeles Fair Chance Initiative for Hiring and the Los Angeles
County Fair Chance Ordinance both add requirements on top of state law,
including notice posted in job ads and extra recordkeeping. If a job is in
the city or the unincorporated county, those rules may apply too.

## If you think an employer broke these rules

You can file a complaint with the California Civil Rights Department. In the
City of Los Angeles you can also contact the Office of Wage Standards, and in
unincorporated Los Angeles County the Department of Consumer and Business
Affairs.

There are deadlines for filing. Do not wait.

## Cleaning up your record

You may be able to petition to dismiss or reduce an old conviction, or to
seal an arrest record. Free help with this exists — look under the ID and
documents category in this app for record clearing clinics.
